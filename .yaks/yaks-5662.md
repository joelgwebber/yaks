Found auditing `yaks sheds` from ~/src/fs/subtext/surveyor on 2026-10-16. The primary is on `kinds-discovery`, all the work happens on that branch, and threads land into it. Joel: "for most of this last arc the yak diffs have just seemed off in ways I can't quite put my finger on."

Four separate causes, one child each:
- `landed` treats a later edit made by the REFERENCE (main re-parenting a yak) as an unlanded edit in every shed that once touched that yak, so stale sheds keep listing work that is not theirs.
- The primary is measured from `origin/main` even when it is on another branch, so `main` lists the whole branch arc.
- The fork point is the oldest HEAD reflog entry, which predates a branch switch: a clone that starts on `main` and checks out the working branch is credited with the whole branch (and with main's claims: `shaving:` + `active`).
- A shed whose HEAD is already in the primary and whose farm has nothing outstanding reads plain `idle`, with no hint why it is still around (uncommitted code, untracked files).

Evidence (2026-10-16): every clone's HEAD was an ancestor of the primary's. review-survey-e0c0-arc listed survey-209f/b0fd/d13d/eb0c as outstanding only because main's 10da8d7 re-parented them under survey-3237. fix-collapse-on-journals listed survey-e3f7 because main's 52cf335 dropped its parent. rzzejj28kv0t's HEAD reflog starts at c431937 (main) and logs `checkout: moving from main to kinds-discovery` 3 minutes later.

Related: yaks-c41f (multiple branches per shed) is the broader design question; this family fixes the measurements without taking that on.
